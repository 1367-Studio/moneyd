Crypto markets trade every hour of every day and can move 10% before most people have finished breakfast. The moves look random, but most of them come back to a handful of forces. Knowing them helps you tell a headline that matters from noise.

## 1. Global liquidity and interest rates

Crypto behaves like a high-risk asset. When interest rates fall and money is cheap, investors take more risk and crypto tends to rise. When central banks raise rates or keep them high, the opposite usually happens. That is why bitcoin often reacts to US inflation data and Federal Reserve meetings, just like technology stocks.

## 2. Bitcoin sets the direction

Bitcoin is the largest and most traded crypto asset, and most other tokens move with it, only more sharply. When bitcoin falls 5%, many smaller tokens fall 10% or more. Traders watch **bitcoin dominance**, bitcoin's share of the total crypto market value, to see whether money is flowing into or out of riskier tokens.

## 3. Supply and the halving

Bitcoin's supply is fixed by its code: there will never be more than 21 million coins. New coins are created as a reward for the miners who process transactions, and that reward is cut in half roughly every four years (every 210,000 blocks). This event is called the **halving**. It reduces the flow of new coins coming to market, and past halvings have been followed by large price swings, though the timing and size have varied a lot and past patterns are no guarantee.

## 4. Fund flows and ETFs

Exchange-traded funds that hold bitcoin and ether give traditional investors an easy way in. Daily **ETF inflows and outflows** have become one of the most watched numbers in crypto: steady inflows mean new demand, while outflows show investors pulling back. Large holders, such as companies that keep bitcoin on their balance sheets, also move prices when they announce big purchases or sales.

## 5. Leverage and liquidations

Many crypto traders borrow to increase the size of their bets. When prices move against them, exchanges automatically close their positions; these forced sales are called **liquidations**. They can create chain reactions: falling prices trigger liquidations, which push prices lower, which trigger more liquidations. This is why crypto sell-offs are often fast and deep, and why the same thing can happen in reverse during a rally (a "short squeeze").

## 6. Regulation

Decisions by regulators can move the whole market: approval of new investment products, lawsuits against exchanges, tax rules, or new frameworks such as the EU's MiCA regulation. Clear rules tend to bring in institutional money; crackdowns or uncertainty tend to push it away.

## 7. Hacks and failures

Security incidents are among the fastest-moving crypto stories. When an exchange or protocol is hacked, the damage goes beyond the stolen funds:

- Users rush to withdraw from other platforms, fearing they might be next.
- Hackers may sell stolen coins, adding selling pressure.
- Regulators and politicians pay closer attention to the sector.

The collapse of the FTX exchange in 2022 showed how one failure can spread across the whole market. When you see a hack headline, watch how much was taken, whether user funds are covered and whether the platform pauses withdrawals.

## 8. Stablecoins

Stablecoins are tokens designed to hold a steady value, usually one US dollar. They are the main way money moves between crypto platforms, so the total value of stablecoins in circulation is a rough gauge of how much cash is ready to buy crypto. A stablecoin losing its peg is a serious warning sign.

## Reading a crypto headline

When a crypto story lands, ask:

- Is this about the whole market (rates, regulation, a major hack) or one token?
- Does it change supply or demand, or just sentiment?
- Is leverage likely to make the move bigger than the news justifies?

## Key takeaways

- Crypto trades like a high-risk asset and reacts to interest rates and liquidity.
- Bitcoin leads; most other tokens follow with bigger swings.
- ETF flows, leverage and liquidations explain many of the sharpest moves.
- Hacks, failures and regulatory action are risk signals that can hit the entire market. Follow them in the [Crypto section](/crypto).

Crypto assets are highly volatile and you can lose all the money you put in.
